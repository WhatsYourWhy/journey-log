  • Task Hydra

    I have too many tasks and I cannot tell where to start.

    Tap to open the ritual

    A short ritual for overwhelm spirals. Use it when the list keeps multiplying and you need one visible task instead of ten competing ones.

    The oath

    I choose one head. I do not grow new ones.

    Today's three quests are visible. The rest can wait.

    How to use it

    1. Name one task, not an entire project category.
    2. Gather only the tools that belong to that task.
    3. Put every new idea in a parking lot instead of promoting it into the current battle.
    4. Finish, stop, and choose again.

    Minimal prompt

    Single-Task Oath
I choose one head. I do not grow new ones.
Today's three quests: [   ] [   ] [   ]
Parking lot: ___________________________

    Why it helps

    • It reduces switching costs.
    • It creates an external rule before overwhelm starts negotiating.
    • It keeps new ideas from hijacking the current lane.

  • Dopamine Goblin

    Shiny distractions keep outbidding the work I chose.

    Tap to open the ritual

    A negotiation ritual for shiny distractions. Use it when the boring task keeps losing the auction to whatever feels more stimulating right now.

    How to use it

    1. Name the distraction out loud. Writing it down takes away half its power.
    2. Pre-choose the reward: a snack, a scroll, a song, a stretch. Decide now, not later.
    3. Set a short timer (15-25 minutes). Work the boring task until it rings.
    4. When the timer rings, take the reward. Fully. No guilt. Then decide: another round or done.

    Minimal prompt

    Goblin Deal
The shiny thing: ___________________________
The boring task: ___________________________
Pre-chosen reward: ___________________________
Timer: ___ minutes
Deal struck: yes / no

    Why it helps

    • It stops the craving from being the villain and makes it part of the contract.
    • It gives the brain a concrete finish line instead of open-ended suffering.
    • It adds friction to the impulse without pretending the impulse is fake.

  • Cave Bear

    I keep retreating, hiding, or staying in the cave even when I want out.

    Tap to open the ritual

    A micro-transition ritual for shutdown and retreat. Use it when the cave feels safer than the world and you need one doorway, not an expedition.

    How to use it

    1. Stand in a literal or figurative doorway: the bedroom door, the reply button, the first sentence of the email.
    2. Take three slow breaths in the threshold.
    3. Make three small moves: sit up, feet on floor, stand. Or: open app, read one line, type one word.
    4. From the doorway, decide: one more step, or rest here. Either answer is legal.

    Minimal prompt

    Threshold Spell
Doorway I am crossing: ___________________________
Three breaths: done / not yet
Three moves: [   ] [   ] [   ]
From the doorway: one more step / rest here

    Why it helps

    • It shrinks the ask from "rejoin the world" to "reach the door."
    • It uses body movement to interrupt the freeze loop.
    • It gives you a decision point after motion, not before it.

  • Perfection Wyrm

    I keep polishing the work instead of finishing or sending it.

    Tap to open the ritual

    A counter-spell for perfectionism loops. Use it when the Wyrm whispers that the draft is not ready and you know it never will be.

    The oath

    My first job is existence, not elegance.

    I will make the whole shape before I polish any sentence.

    How to use it

    1. Set a timer for the entire draft, not the first section.
    2. Write every section as bullet points, placeholders, or garbage prose. All of it counts.
    3. Mark anything that feels wrong with [FIX LATER] and keep moving.
    4. When the timer ends, you have a full ugly shape. Now you may polish.

    Minimal prompt

    Ugly First Pass
My first job is existence, not elegance.
Sections to rough out: [   ] [   ] [   ] [   ]
[FIX LATER] count: ___
Ship line: when __________ is done, I send it.

    Why it helps

    • It separates creation from editing, which use different brain modes.
    • It removes the Wyrm's leverage: you cannot endlessly revise what does not exist yet.
    • It makes "done" visible before "perfect" gets a vote.

  • Rejection Wisp

    One hard message makes the whole inbox feel dangerous.

    Tap to open the ritual

    A pre-written response ritual for rejection sensitivity. Use it when one hard message has turned the whole inbox into a minefield.

    How to use it

    1. Pick one reply shape: acknowledge, yes, no, or clarify.
    2. Fill the slots with the minimum honest words. No backstory, no apology tour.
    3. Read it once for facts. If the facts are right, send it.
    4. Close the thread. You replied. The rest is not your job right now.

    Minimal prompt

    Reply Scaffold
Shape: acknowledge / yes / no / clarify
Slot 1 (the fact): ___________________________
Slot 2 (the action): ___________________________
Read once for facts: done / not yet
Send.

    Why it helps

    • It removes the blank-page problem from emotional replies.
    • It separates "what do I say" from "what does this mean about me."
    • It lets you act before the Wisp finishes narrating.

  • Sensory Storm

    Everything around me feels too loud, bright, sharp, or intense.

    Tap to open the ritual

    A sensory triage ritual for overload. Use it when sound, light, and texture are piling up and your brain needs less input before it can do anything else.

    How to use it

    1. Name the loudest, brightest, scratchiest, or most socially demanding input right now.
    2. Lower or remove that one thing. Headphones off, lights down, door closed, notification silenced.
    3. Give your body one steady anchor: cold water, pressure, a single calm sound, or dimmer light.
    4. Wait two minutes. Then ask what your brain can handle from here.

    Minimal prompt

    Reduce Inputs First
Loudest input right now: ___________________________
Action to lower it: ___________________________
One-sense anchor: ___________________________
After 2 min, I can handle: ___________________________

    Why it helps

    • It treats overload as an input problem, not a willpower problem.
    • It works faster than any strategy that adds more things to manage.
    • It gives the nervous system room to come back online before you ask it to perform.

  • Burnout Dragon

    Everything costs too much and I do not have the fuel I used to.

    Tap to open the ritual

    A survival ritual for burnout. Use it when everything costs too much and the honest answer is a smaller day, not a better attitude.

    The oath

    I will name what must happen. Everything else is optional until proven otherwise.

    One recovery action goes on the list before a single new task does.

    How to use it

    1. List only what truly must happen today. If it can wait until tomorrow without real consequences, it waits.
    2. Add one recovery action to the list: a nap, a walk, a meal, silence. It is not optional.
    3. Cross off anything you added from habit, guilt, or fantasy fuel.
    4. Do the surviving list in any order. Stop when it is done.

    Minimal prompt

    Minimum Viable Day
Must happen: [   ] [   ] [   ]
Recovery action: ___________________________
Cut from habit/guilt: ___________________________
When the list is done, I stop.

    Why it helps

    • It matches the plan to the actual fuel supply instead of yesterday's capacity.
    • It protects recovery from being the first thing cut.
    • It lets you finish a day instead of failing a bigger one.
